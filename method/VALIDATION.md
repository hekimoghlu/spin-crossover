## 0. Why noise-derived numbers differ

- Every device number depends on random drift and shot draws; this code uses its own seeds, so single-draw quantities (the deployed fit `rmsPTc`, `corrA/B`, `maxPTc`, per-arm calibration rows `surr*`) scatter by tens of per cent between draws even with identical method.
- The dispatched set is a function of that one fit (band, threshold distances), so it differs in size and membership (here vs the paper's 53); all `u*`, `cl*`, `routed*`, cost-of-dispatched-set numbers inherit that difference (e.g. how many successful designs sit in the dispatched set fixes the raw-arm 'wrong' counts).
- Unstated details chosen here (ASSUMPTIONS.md): noiseless basis-change gates and state preparation, independent raw/mitigated histograms, folding-gate selection seed, readout correction with the exact published flip rate, drift only on the two-qubit error. These plausibly explain part of the differences in residual mitigation bias on the SWAP-routed platforms (ours smaller by ~5-12 K) and in the raw Cepheus calibration-compound bias (ours ~25 % larger); this was not isolated further.
- Implemented stack (sec. 6.3-6.7): the decoder, surrogate, proposer, escalation policy and QAOA objective have unstated architectures, hyper-parameters, penalty grids and objective constants; the choices made here are listed in ASSUMPTIONS.md 28-33. QAOA in particular depends entirely on the unstated value/cost/budget/penalty definition, so P(optimal) and the approximation ratio are not comparable in absolute terms. The surrogate study uses one independent noise draw (epoch 1); its line fit gives 4 K held-out, a single-draw quantity.
- No parameter was tuned to approach the paper's values.
